1. Master Schedule Board Primary Tool

File: master_schedule_board.html (project root)

Purpose: The main scheduling workspace. Loads the engine's output (schedule_solution_v3.json) and lets you drag course sections between periods, with a built-in cascade solver that predicts how each move ripples through every affected student's schedule.

Getting Started

  1. Open master_schedule_board.html in any modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox).
  2. You will see a load screen with a drop zone. Drag your schedule_solution_v3.json file onto the drop zone, or click to browse for it.
  3. The board loads: a scoreboard appears at the top, the period grid fills with section cards, and a detail panel opens on the right.

Understanding the Layout

  • Scoreboard (top): Shows four KPIs — Conflicts, Placed students, Moves made, and Locks active. An arc gauge shows overall placement rate. Below it, a grade-level breakdown shows conflicts per grade (9/10/11/12).
  • Toolbar: Contains 8 filter controls (Term, Dept, Teacher, Room, Grade, Course, Fill, Search), plus buttons for Undo, Save Session, Load Session, and Export. A move counter shows how many changes you've made.
  • Board (center): Seven columns, one per period (A through G). Each column contains section cards.
  • Detail Panel (right): Shows information about the selected section or student.

Section Cards

Each card represents one course section and shows:

  • Course code and section number (e.g., "810 §1")
  • Course title
  • Teacher name
  • Enrollment vs. capacity (e.g., "22/25")
  • Term badge (FY = full year, S1 = semester 1, S2 = semester 2)
  • A red dot if the section has student conflicts
  • A lock icon (click to lock/unlock)

Cards are color-coded by department along their left border.

Moving a Section (Drag and Drop)

  1. Grab any unlocked card and drag it toward a different period column.
  2. As you hover over a target column, a Move Preview tooltip appears showing the cascade analysis:
    • How many enrolled students are affected
    • How many students can be automatically relocated to other sections (✓)
    • How many students would gain a new conflict (✗)
    • How many previously-conflicted students could now be placed (↑)
    • Net impact: the total change in conflict count
    • Teacher and room conflict warnings
  3. Drop the card into the target column to apply the move. The board updates immediately — students are relocated, conflict counts recalculate, and the scoreboard refreshes.
Tip: Look for moves with a negative net impact — those reduce conflicts. The preview shows this before you commit.

Clicking a Section

Click a card (without dragging) to see its details in the right panel:

  • Section metadata (course code, title, teacher, room, term, capacity)
  • Teacher's full schedule grid (all periods × semesters)
  • Room's full schedule grid
  • List of enrolled students (clickable names)
  • Unscheduled requests for that course

Student View

  1. Click any student name in the enrolled students list (inside the detail panel) to enter student view.
  2. You will see:
    • A breadcrumb trail (Section → Student) — click to go back
    • The student's full 7×2 schedule grid (periods A-G × S1/S2)
    • A list of their enrolled courses, each showing alternative sections available
    • Conflicts highlighted in red
  3. For each course, alternatives are listed with their period, section number, and enrollment. If an alternative has no scheduling conflict, a green Move button appears.
  4. Click Move to reassign that student to the alternative section. The student's schedule updates instantly.

Locking Sections

  • Click the lock icon on any card to lock it. Locked sections cannot be dragged.
  • Click again to unlock.
  • The scoreboard shows how many sections are currently locked.
  • If you try to drag a locked section, nothing happens — this prevents accidental moves on sections you've already finalized.

Undo

  • Click the Undo button or press Ctrl+Z to reverse the most recent move.
  • Undo works for both section moves (with full cascade rollback) and student moves.
  • You can undo repeatedly all the way back to the original loaded state.

Filtering

Use the toolbar dropdowns to narrow what's visible on the board:

FilterWhat It Does
TermShow only Full Year, Semester 1, or Semester 2 sections
DeptShow only sections from one department (English, Math, Science, etc.)
TeacherShow only one teacher's sections
RoomShow only sections assigned to one room
GradeShow only sections that have enrolled students in that grade level
CourseShow only sections of one specific course (e.g., "810 Theology 9")
FillShow sections by enrollment fill: >80%, <50%, Full, or Empty
SearchFree text search across course code, title, teacher, and room

Filters combine — setting Teacher + Grade shows only that teacher's sections that have students in that grade. Click Clear Filters to reset all filters at once.

Save & Load Session

  • Save Session: Downloads a .json file containing all your moves and locked sections. You can close the browser and come back later.
  • Load Session: Opens a saved session file. The board loads the original solution data, then replays your saved moves in order. Locks are restored.
Important: A saved session replays moves against the same schedule_solution_v3.json you originally loaded. If you re-run the engine and get a new solution file, old sessions may not replay correctly.

Export

Click Export to see a full text summary of all moves you've made, including cascade details for each section move and individual student moves. You can copy this to share with colleagues or paste into a report.

2. Singleton Board Scheduling Tool

File: boards/singleton_board.html (also singleton_board.html in project root)

Purpose: A specialized grid for scheduling singleton courses — courses that have only one section school-wide. Because there is only one section, every student who needs the course must fit into the same period, making placement critical.

Understanding the Layout

  • Toolbar (top): Shows stats — total singletons, moves made, conflict-free count, locked count, and conflicts. Buttons for Save, Commit, Reset, and Export.
  • Filter bar: Filter by department, conflict status, and free-text search. A color legend shows what each card color means.
  • Grid: A large table with teachers as rows (grouped by department) and period/semester slots as columns (A through G, each split into S1/S2/FY = 21 columns). Teachers are listed on the left with a sticky column so they stay visible when scrolling.

Card Colors

ColorMeaning
RedSingleton course — draggable
Green backgroundRegular (non-singleton) course — shown for context, not draggable
Padlock iconLocked section — cannot be moved
Red outlineTeacher conflict (double-booked in same period/semester)

Moving a Singleton

  1. Find the singleton card (red) in the grid.
  2. Drag it to a different period/semester cell in the same teacher row.
  3. An Impact Analysis modal appears showing:
    • Current enrollment
    • How many students would gain a conflict at the new period
    • How many students could newly enroll (their schedule is free at the new period)
    • Projected enrollment after the move
    • Lists of specifically affected students
  4. Click Accept to confirm or Reject to cancel.

Other Features

  • Click a card to see a popover with section details: teacher, room, period, enrollment bar, and student roster.
  • Lock singletons you've finalized to prevent accidental moves.
  • Change Log (floating panel): tracks every move with individual undo and a running net impact tally.
  • Zoom: Use the zoom controls (50%–100%) to adjust the grid size if it's too wide for your screen.
  • Export: Download as CSV, JSON, or a changes-only JSON file.
Tip: Use the department filter to focus on one department at a time — the full grid with all teachers can be overwhelming.

3. Conflict Resolution Console Scheduling Tool

File: boards/conflict_resolution_console.html

Purpose: A course-level view of all scheduling conflicts with AI-generated fix recommendations for each course. Use this to decide which courses need more sections, different period placements, or other adjustments before re-running the engine.

Understanding the Layout

  • Stats bar (top): Shows total unresolved conflicts, placement rate, placed/total students, and students affected.
  • Tabs: Filter courses by conflict severity:
    • High Impact — 5 or more conflicts (fix these first)
    • Medium — 3-4 conflicts
    • Low — 1-2 conflicts
    • By Period — view by period distribution
    • Approved — fixes you've already approved
    • All Courses — everything
  • Course cards: Expandable accordion cards for each affected course.
  • Floating dock (bottom): Shows approved count and estimated conflicts resolved.

Using a Course Card

  1. Click a course card to expand it.
  2. Inside you'll see:
    • Info grid: Current number of sections, which periods they cover, which periods have gaps, teacher names, teacher free periods, and teacher load
    • Period dots: Visual dots showing A-G, filled for covered periods and empty for gaps
    • Top blocking courses: The courses that are causing conflicts for this course's students
    • Fix recommendation: A green (low-risk) or orange (moderate-risk) action box describing what the engine recommends
  3. Click Approve to accept the recommendation or Reject to dismiss it.

Batch Actions

  • Approve All High-Impact: Approves every fix in the High Impact tab at once.
  • Clear: Resets all approvals.
  • Export: Copies a text summary of all approved decisions to your clipboard. Paste this into a document or share it with your team.
Note: Approving a fix here does not change the engine data. It records your decision — you then apply the approved changes to your templates and re-run the engine.

4. Student Conflict Report Scheduling Tool

File: boards/student_conflict_report.html

Purpose: A student-level view of every scheduling conflict. Each student with a conflict gets a personal schedule grid where you can drag courses between period/semester slots to resolve their conflicts manually.

Understanding the Layout

  • Toolbar: Stats (students, conflicts, resolved, moves), Export buttons (CSV, JSON, Changes JSON).
  • Filter bar: Grade filter, department filter, name/ID search, expand/collapse all, zoom controls.
  • Summary cards: Total conflicts, students affected, breakdown by grade, resolution rate.
  • Student cards: One expandable card per student with conflicts.

Working with a Student Card

  1. Click a student card to expand it.
  2. Inside you'll see a schedule grid (7 periods × 2 semesters) with course tiles:
    • Green tiles = successfully placed courses
    • Red tiles = conflicted courses (not placed)
    • Blue tiles = courses you've moved this session
  3. Below the grid, an Unscheduled section lists bumped courses with available alternative sections shown as clickable pills.

Resolving a Conflict

  1. Drag a course tile from one period/semester cell to another in the student's grid.
  2. An Impact Analysis modal appears showing enrollment changes and affected students.
  3. Click Accept to apply or Reject to cancel.
  4. Alternatively, click an alternative section pill in the Unscheduled area to place a bumped course into a free slot.

Other Features

  • Click a tile to see a popover with section details, enrollment bar, and student roster.
  • Change Log (floating panel): Tracks all moves with individual undo.
  • Zoom: Adjust the schedule grid size (50%–100%) for comfort.
  • Export: Three formats — CSV (all data), JSON (all data), or Changes JSON (only your moves).
Tip: Start with Grade 12 students — their graduation requirements make their conflicts the most critical to resolve.

5. Request Recommendations Scheduling Tool

File: boards/student_request_recommendations.html

Purpose: Provides concrete alternative options for every student whose course request was bumped (unscheduled). For each bumped request, the board suggests either adding an alternative course, swapping with an existing course, or explains when no resolution is possible.

Understanding the Tabs

TabWhat It Shows
Add AlternativeStudents who have a free period where an alternative section exists — they can add the course without dropping anything
Swap CourseStudents whose schedule is full but could swap one course for another to resolve the bump
No ResolutionStudents where no viable alternative exists — requires engine-level changes (more sections, different period placement)
ApprovedRecommendations you've approved this session
AllEverything combined

Using a Recommendation Card

  1. Expand a student card to see the bumped course, the blocking course, and which periods are filled.
  2. Review the option cards:
    • ADD options: Show the alternative course, its period, teacher, and current enrollment. Click Approve to accept.
    • SWAP options: Show what you'd get and what you'd drop. A red warning appears if the course being dropped is a core/graduation requirement.
  3. Click Approve on the option you prefer.

Batch Actions

  • Approve All Adds: Approves every "Add Alternative" recommendation at once (safe since no course is dropped).
  • Export: Generates a text summary of all approved changes, grouped by grade level.
Warning: Be careful with Swap recommendations that drop a graduation requirement. The red warning label flags these — only approve if you are certain the student can meet graduation requirements another way.

6. Credit Validation Report Report

File: boards/credit_validation_report.html

Purpose: Identifies students whose course requests exceed the 35-credit cap (7 periods × 5.0 credits). The engine halts until these violations are resolved. The report ranks each student's courses by priority to show which ones to drop.

Understanding the Display

  • A red "Engine Halted" banner appears at the top, explaining that the engine cannot proceed until these violations are cleared.
  • Each student card shows:
    • Student name, ID, grade, and how many credits they are over the cap
    • A credit bar showing total credits vs. the 35-credit cap (the red overshoot is the excess)
    • A course table listing every requested course with its priority level (P1 through P5) and credits
    • Green-highlighted rows are drop candidates — P1 (elective) courses that can be removed first

Priority Levels

PriorityMeaningDrop?
P1 ElectiveElective course, lowest priorityDrop first
P2 PathwayPart of a course pathwayDrop if needed
P3 DepartmentDepartment requirementConsult counselor
P4 CoreCore graduation requirementAvoid dropping
P5 Never DropMandatory — cannot be removedNever drop

Actions

  • Search: Find a specific student by name or ID.
  • Sort: Reorder by most/least over cap, name, or number of courses.
  • Export CSV: Download the violations list for counselor review.
What to do: For each student, remove enough P1/P2 courses from their request list (in Template 8) to bring their total to 35 credits or below. Then re-run the engine.

7. Constraint Builder Scheduling Tool

File: boards/constraint_builder.html

Purpose: A form-based tool for creating scheduling constraints (semester locks, period locks, co-schedule rules, exclusions) that translate into engine parameters. Use this to define rules before running the engine.

Understanding the Layout

  • Left panel — Active Constraints: Shows all constraints you've created. Each card has a type badge, course chips, rules summary, and Edit/Remove buttons.
  • Right panel — Structured Form tab: The form to create or edit a constraint.
  • Right panel — Engine Rules tab: Shows the engine parameters generated by your constraints as formatted pseudo-code.

Constraint Types

TypeWhat It DoesExample
Semester LockForces specific courses into S1 or S2 onlyAP Econ Micro = S1, AP Econ Macro = S2
Full FreedomLets the engine place courses freely with no restrictionsTheology courses — engine picks best periods
Prescribed SplitAssigns specific sections to specific semesters3 sections in S1, 3 sections in S2
Period LockForces a course into a specific periodBand always in Period G
ExclusionPrevents two courses from being in the same periodAP Chem and AP Bio cannot overlap

Creating a Constraint

  1. Enter a name for the constraint (e.g., "AP Economics Pair").
  2. Select a type from the dropdown.
  3. Click course chips in the course picker to select which courses are affected. Courses are organized by department.
  4. Configure the type-specific options (semester assignments, toggle switches for engine behavior).
  5. Click Apply Constraint. The constraint appears in the left panel.

Viewing Engine Rules

Switch to the Engine Rules tab to see the engine parameters generated by all your active constraints. These are the values you would enter into the engine's configuration.

Tip: The board comes pre-loaded with two example constraints (AP Economics Pair and Theology Paired Courses) to show how the form works.

8. Course Request Workbook Scheduling Tool

File: boards/master_schedule_builder.html

Purpose: A multi-tab workbook for managing student course requests. Validates prerequisites, checks credit caps, flags duplicates, and organizes data by grade level. This is used before running the engine to ensure clean input data.

Tabs

TabWhat It Shows
Master WorksheetAll students, all course requests, with validation status
Course TallyPer-course and per-department request counts with totals
Manual ReviewStudents flagged for counselor review (badge shows count)
Grade 9 / 10 / 11 / 12Grade-specific views (badge shows student count per grade)

Validation Indicators

  • Green PASS pill — prerequisite met
  • Red FAIL pill — prerequisite not met (needs counselor override or course removal)
  • Gray N/A pill — no prerequisite required
  • Flag tags: PREREQ (prerequisite issue), TRANSCRIPT (transcript mismatch), UNKNOWN (unrecognized course), DUPLICATE (same course requested twice), CREDIT (credit cap exceeded)

Key Actions

  • Import Data: Load a CSV or TXT file of student course requests.
  • Add Student: Manually enter a new student with name, ID, grade, and course selections.
  • Export CSV: Download the validated request data.
  • Print: Print all sheets in a print-optimized layout.
  • Search & Filter: Find students by name/ID, or show only flagged students.
Note: Resolve all red FAIL pills and flag tags before running the engine. The Manual Review tab collects every student that needs attention.

9. Data Source Audit Report Reference

File: boards/data_source_audit_report.html

Purpose: A read-only report documenting data quality findings across all 6 input files. Lists critical, high, medium, and low severity issues with specific action items. Use this as a checklist before uploading data to the engine.

How to Use

  1. Open the report and review the Action Items box at the top — these are the 12 things that must be fixed before re-uploading data.
  2. Click each file section to expand it and see the specific findings for that file.
  3. Work through the action items in order of severity (Critical first, then High).

Files Audited

  1. Course Sectioning Template
  2. Student Course Requests
  3. LEO II Cohorts
  4. Tenant Intake / School Setup
  5. Prior Year Master Schedule
  6. Historical Grades

This is a static reference document — there are no interactive controls beyond expanding/collapsing sections.

11. Recommended Workflow

The boards are designed to be used in a specific order. Here is the recommended sequence for a full scheduling cycle:

1 Data Source Audit Report — Review data quality. Fix all critical and high issues in your template files.

2 Credit Validation Report — Resolve students over the 35-credit cap. Remove excess P1/P2 courses from Template 8.

3 Course Request Workbook — Import and validate all student course requests. Resolve prerequisite failures and flags. Export clean data.

4 Constraint Builder — Define semester locks, period locks, and co-schedule rules before running the engine.

5 Run the engine (python schedule_engine_v3.py) — generates schedule_solution_v3.json.

6 Conflict Resolution Console — Review course-level conflicts and approve fix recommendations.

7 Singleton Board — Focus on singleton courses. Move them to periods that reduce student conflicts.

8 Master Schedule Board — Load the solution. Drag sections between periods, use cascade preview to find improvements. Save your session.

9 Student Conflict Report — Drill into individual students with remaining conflicts. Reassign them to alternative sections.

10 Request Recommendations — Review and approve alternative course suggestions for any remaining bumped requests.

11 Apply changes to templates, re-run engine, repeat until conflicts are at an acceptable level.

Tip: You don't have to use every board every time. For quick adjustments, the Master Schedule Board (step 8) is often enough on its own. Use the other boards when you need to dig deeper into specific issues.
